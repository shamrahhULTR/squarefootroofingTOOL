How to use this manual

The whole visit, in order, every appointment. Learn it word for word first, then make it your own voice. Read the boxed lines out loud. Those are the lines that close. Everything else is how you get there.

D.F.W.T.S. Don't F*** With The System. Every step below is in order for a reason. Skip a step and you skip the sale. Run all ten, every appointment, even when it feels like you don't need to. Especially then.
RULE 01
Control the room and you control the close.
RULE 02
Move fast in your head. Go slow in front of them.
RULE 03
Say "easy pay" 3 times before you ever say a price.
RULE 04
The decision is today. A right call tonight is right tomorrow.
RULE 05
Whoever mentions the project first loses. Let them bring up the roof.

Commitment statements: the thread that runs through everything. A commitment statement collects a small yes. "Fair enough?" "Is that important to you?" "Would you settle for anything less?" "Now you see why people love Square Foot Roofing." Stack them all night and the close is just the last yes in a long row of yeses. They're marked COMMITMENT below. New reps: these are the parts you do not skip.

OS

The Operating System

Why every step exists

Before the word tracks, the psychology. Every lost deal traces back to one of five reasons, and every step of this system exists to kill one of them:

The "No"What it really meansWhere you kill it
No trustThey don't believe you yet.Entry & Warm-Up
No needThey don't feel the problem.Needs Assessment
No interestThey checked out. You stopped involving them.Every step. Keep them talking
No hurryThe pain isn't urgent.Inspection & photos
No moneyThey don't see the payback.Options, Planner & the math

Three questions every customer must have answered before they buy: Why do I have to buy from you? Why do I have to buy now? Why should I pay more for this? If your presentation answers all three, you never push. They close themselves.

The four stages of learning this system:

STAGE 1
Unconsciously bad. Losing, and you don't know why.
STAGE 2
Consciously bad. You see the leaks. Good.
STAGE 3
Consciously good. Clean, but it takes effort.
STAGE 4
Unconsciously good. Runs like breathing.

The logical conclusion to a masterful presentation is the sale. And it starts with knowing your product cold: every component in the system, every warranty term, every number on the Investment Planner. Confidence breeds competence, and product knowledge is where the confidence comes from. You don't run this live until you can run it clean. Role-play every step with a teammate and drill the objection engine until an objection feels like an invitation.

01

The Entry

Disarm · Look pro · Earn the look

They're watching from the window before you reach the porch. The sale starts in the driveway, so show up like you do this every day.

  • Park on the street, never block them in. Truck clean and visible.
  • Walk up light. Leave the tablet in the truck. Heavy gear at the door makes you look like every other salesman.
  • Read the roof on your way in with your eyes, not your camera. No pictures yet, that's what the inspection is for. A quick note on the clipboard is plenty. Caring is a posture before it's a word.
  • Knock, step back a step and down a step, smile before the door opens.
Say it at the door

"Mr. and Mrs. [Name]? [Your name] with Square Foot Roofing, good to meet you."

(Shake hands, smile, and get them talking. You're figuring out why you're there without having to ask: "Beautiful spot out here. How long have you two been in the home?" If it doesn't come up on its own, it's fine to ask: "So what got you two looking at the roof?" Walk the outside with them, eyes up, notes where they can see. Save the camera and the measurements for the inspection. Then, back inside:)

"Appreciate you having me in. Here's how I work. I'm looking at two things tonight: what you want done and what you need done. If I can help, I'll show you the options and an exact price locked for a full year. Either way you'll have everything you need to make a smart decision. Fair enough?"

If they push back at the door ("we just need a price," "we're not doing anything until next year," "make it quick"), relax. That's curiosity talking, not rejection. Don't argue and don't speed up. Five good minutes buys you the next five.

Resistance at the door · The humble line lives here, and only here

"Totally understand, and I'm not here to do a big song and dance. Honestly, I don't even know yet if I'm the right fit for what you need, and I can't give you a real number until I've actually looked at the roof, because a guess would only waste your time. Let me take a real look, and if it's not for us, no hard feelings. Fair?"

(Never volunteer "I don't know if I can help you yet" at a friendly door. It plants doubt where there wasn't any. It's a tool for lowering a raised guard, not an opener.)

Seat them right: both decision-makers side by side, facing you, your back to a wall. Never a TV or busy window behind you. Set the seats every single time. It's free and almost nobody does it.

02

The Warm-Up

Two ears · One mouth · No selling

Ten to fifteen minutes, and the customer is in control. This is not the time to sell anything. It's the time to listen and learn. People love talking about themselves, so give them every chance to. Ask as many questions as you can. Sooner or later they'll tell you how and why they buy things, and that's the map to the whole sale. Every question you ask is also a brick of trust.

Walk in curious. The house is full of conversation starters, outside and in:

  • Outside: the garden, the boat, the truck, the grill, the flag, the workshop, the basketball hoop, a fresh paint job. "Who's the gardener?" "You take that boat out on the bay?"
  • Inside: family photos, trophies, diplomas, team gear, a renovated kitchen, the dog. "Is that your daughter graduating?" "Who's the Cowboys fan?"
  • Whatever they light up about, layer it: "How long you been doing that?" "How'd you get into it?"

Do

  • Ask about the home, how long they've lived there, what they love about the area
  • Use their names. A person's name is the sweetest sound in any language
  • Listen more than you talk, and let silence do some work
  • Match their pace and temperament

Never talk about

  • Religion
  • Politics
  • Sex
  • Your company (not yet)
  • Your product (not yet)
  • Yourself. Nobody buys a roof because of your fishing trip

Read who you're selling. People land in one of four temperaments. Spot it early and adjust:

Aggressive
Wants the point. Cut the fluff, give options, let them decide.
Expressive
Wants the story. Be warm, paint the picture, keep energy up.
Passive
Wants safety. Slow down, reassure, never pressure.
Analytical
Wants proof. Bring numbers, specs, and warranties in writing.
Bridge questions · Whoever mentions the project first loses

Don't bring up the roof. Ask questions that make them bring it up:

"So how did you hear about us?"

"What made you decide to look into the project now?"

"How long have you two been in the home?"

"And how long have you been thinking about getting this done?"

(Write all four down word for word. The first two are the buying motive for the whole night. Years in the home tells you how much emotion is attached to the house. And years thinking about the project is a closing tool you'll cash in later: "You've been living with this for three years. Has it gotten any better?")

How do you know the warm-up is over? Long pauses, shifting body language, or "okay, let's get down to business." Then move, and not before.

03

Needs Assessment

Survey first · Then inspect

This is where they decide if you're credible. Confirm three things without making it a checklist: are they the homeowners, are all decision-makers home, and what will matter most when they make this decision. Write their answers down. It shows you care.

Layer every answer (L.Q.R. Listen, Question, Respond)

"Tell me more about that." · "How long has that been going on?" · "Is that important to you? Why?" · "How would it feel to just have that handled?"

Transition to the inspection

"Now that I understand what you have in mind, let's take a closer look at what actually needs to be done, so I can tell you honestly whether we can help. Would you mind joining me and showing me what you've been seeing?"

The attic is where a roof tells the truth. Get up where they never look: leaks, daylight through the deck, soft or rotted wood, mold, bad ventilation, nail pops. Bring them along and let them see it with their own eyes. Build it in this order: Problem, Cause, what it's doing to their house.

  • When you find something real, let it land: "Wow. Come look at this."
  • If they don't bring up something you found, it's your job to bring it up.
  • Photograph everything you find. Those pictures are your evidence for the kitchen table.
  • Code Red (active leak, daylight, rot, mold, sagging): say it out loud. Now "today" isn't pushy. It's the only smart answer.

Back inside, the photos come out. Right after the inspection, or as soon as you sit back down, pull them up on the tablet and walk the homeowners through what you found, worst first. A leak they can see in their own kitchen is real in a way words never are.

Bonus tip · When you find something they had no idea about

"Folks, I need to show you something, because you'd have no way of knowing this from the ground. This is what we call a Code Red. It doesn't wait, and every week it sits it gets more expensive, because now it's not just the roof, it's the wood under it, the insulation, the ceiling. When it's like this we can usually put in a rush order so it gets handled before it turns into three other bills. Let me show you exactly what I found."

When you head back inside, they should be thinking: "We have a real problem and we need it fixed."

Say it exactly like this

05

Options On The Market

Price conditioning · They kill the cheap option

Before you show your system, show the market. Walk them through every option that exists, from doing nothing to the premium national brand. You never insult a competitor by name. You educate, and let the contrast do the work.

Option zero · Do nothing

"Your first option is always the best option if your goal is to spend no money: do nothing. So let me ask, since you started thinking about this work, has the problem gotten better?"

(No. And if there's no visible leak yet: "It's great you're ahead of this. If there were an issue we couldn't see right now and you did nothing, would it get better or worse?")

"That's exactly why doing nothing is always the cheapest option today and the most expensive option tomorrow. So is it safe to say doing nothing is off the table?"

Now walk the market, bottom to top. Roofing prices run per square (a 10x10 area), and the market breaks into three tiers. Use rough ranges for our area and let them see the spread:

The mid-range line

"In the middle you'll find everything average. Average insurance, average materials, average workmanship. And any idea what the enemy of great is? ... Average."

  • The hidden wood trap lives at the bottom of the market: bid low, then hit you on install day with rotted decking at a painful per-sheet price. We inspect first, tell you the worst case up front, and put it in writing.

Then let a neutral third party set the price range, not you. Pull up Homewyse right in front of them. It's not your number, it's the market's number, and that's exactly why it works:

Price conditioning · Homewyse

"Don't take my word for any of this. This is Homewyse, a neutral site that tracks what roofs actually cost. For a home like yours, here's the high end of the market... and here's the cheap end. Pause on that for a second. Wide range, right?"

"Now, that bottom number looks friendly, but that's the corner-cutter: the thin shingles, the uninsured crew, the warranty that dies when the phone gets disconnected. That's the stuff you want to run away from, fast."

"We hit the sweet spot for you. The price that keeps our homeowners happy and referring us, and keeps us in business so we can keep serving the community that serves our homeowners."

(Done right, there is zero sticker shock later. The number lands exactly where the market already told them it would.)

Commitment · The expectation question

"So let me ask, what were you hoping this project was going to cost?"

(Write their number down. If it's low, show them where it lands on the ladder:)

"That expectation lands you right at the small local guy. Now, you told me earlier that [repeat back what they said mattered: warranty, quality, not doing it twice] was important to you. Do you believe you'll find all of that at the lowest price on the market?"

(If they push back:)

"You might. I'm not saying you can't find a needle in a haystack. But if you were a betting man, where do you think you have the highest probability of getting exactly what you're looking for?"

(They point to mid-range or premium. They just moved themselves up the ladder. You didn't.)

First easy-pay seed (1 of 3)

"By the way, you called at a good time. Most of our families don't write one big check. They take care of the whole roof with our easy-pay options for a comfortable amount a month. I'll show you exactly how that works in a bit."

(Say it, then move on. Don't linger.)

Commitment · The value question

"Let me ask you something. When it comes to the roof that protects everything you own, are you shopping for the cheapest price, or the best value for your family?"

(They will say best value. Everyone does. That sentence is the whole sale, and they just said it, not you. If they say it, it's true.)

06

The Demo

Show the system · Hands on the samples

Now show them what a Square Foot roof actually is. Samples in their hands, pictures of real installs, walk the system bottom to top so they understand a roof is a system, not a layer of shingles. No prices yet. The Investment Planner comes later, in the close:

  • Decking: we inspect it, replace what's rotted, and re-secure the whole deck. The right beginning.
  • Waterproof underlayment and ice barrier at the eaves, valleys, and penetrations. Code is the minimum. We don't build to the minimum.
  • Drip edge and flashing around the full perimeter, new pipe collars, no reused rusty metal.
  • The shingle system: show Good, Better, Best side by side. Let them hold the samples. Ask which one they'd want on their home, and which one they think costs more.
  • Ventilation: the part every cheap bid skips, and the part that makes shingles last and keeps the warranty valid. Two projects in one: the roof and the airflow.
  • The warranty, backed by a local company that will still be here to honor it.
Easy-pay seed (2 of 3)

"And whichever level you like, remember what I said earlier: we tailor the monthly to your budget, not the other way around. You'll see it in black and white in a few minutes."

Commitment · Tie it down hard

"Knowing what you know now about this system, would you feel comfortable having it on your home, protecting your family?"

(Yes.)

"Would you settle for anything less?"

07

The Pre-Close

Clear the deck before price

Before you ever show a number, take everything except money off the table. Ask it word for word, then be quiet:

The pre-close question · The biggest commitment of the night

"John and Mary, other than price or payment, is there any reason we couldn't get your project scheduled?"

(If they name something else, solve it, then ask this exact question again the exact same way. When the answer is "no, it's just the money," you're ready to close, and 90% of the work is already done.)

08

The Closing Sequence · The Investment Planner

Same smile · Let them pick · Silence

The best close is a masterful presentation. The second best is assuming the order with confidence. The third best is silence. Keep the exact same energy you've had all night. If you're relaxed, they're relaxed.

Now the Investment Planner comes out. This is the price presentation. It builds their exact roof and shows Good, Better, Best side by side with the easy-pay monthly next to each one. It is not there to defend a number. It's there to get people to pick what they like. So let them pick.

Rep reminder: the discounts are already baked into the Planner's pricing. The number on the screen is already sharpened, so present it with total confidence and never stack extra discounts on top of it. If you flinch at your own price, so will they.

Present the investment

"This covers your entire roof at every level you see here: decking inspection, any bad wood at the per-sheet price we already talked about, ice and water barrier, drip edge, flashing, your shingle in the color you pick, full ventilation, cleanup, and the warranty, all backed by Square Foot. And this price is locked for 365 days, so you're protected from any increase. Fair enough?"

Commitment · Let them pick

"Out of these three, which one do you like for your home?"

(They pick. Cross the other two off the screen or the sheet. Now the whole conversation is about the roof they chose, not whether they're buying one.)

If they flinch at the number, that's an objection, not your cue to defend. Defending early turns an easy yes into a fight. Stay relaxed and keep gathering first: which option they like, how they'd handle the deposit, check or card, whether the monthly fits, where the money is coming from. Every answer you collect shrinks the objection before you ever touch it. Then take the playbook in Step 9 and go down the line, in order. The rebuttal comes last.

T it up. Two simple ways to handle it. Draw the T, show both, then ask which works best:

Cash Plan

One-third deposit by check or card, balance due only when the job is done and you're 100% satisfied.

Easy Pay Plan

Small deposit, then a low monthly amount that nobody in town beats. The Investment Planner shows it live, tailored to their budget.

The close · Easy pay (3 of 3)

"When the time comes, which plan works best for you two?"

(Whichever they pick, cross the other one out. Then lock the details:)

"For the deposit, would you typically write a check or use a card for the points?"

"And the monthly, does that fit the budget if you wanted it to?"

(If no: "How close can you come and still feel comfortable?" Then adjust the option or term in the Investment Planner until it fits. That's the whole point of the tool.)

The today incentive

"Let me ask, when were you hoping to have this done? ... That soon? Okay, this is actually a unique time for us. This is exactly when folks start calling, and what we're doing this month is giving families a reason to get on the schedule now, before the install calendar backs up. Since you're doing this roof anyway, this may be the perfect time to order it and save some money. Let me show you exactly what it does for you."

The straight close · When they're hovering at yes

"John and Mary, we've spent real time together tonight, so let me be straight with you. You need this roof handled, and I want Square Foot to be the one that handles it. If I pack up and you decide later this week, we're scheduling another visit, and that costs us both. So here's my best number: [number], less than [monthly] a month on easy pay. Let's get this done."

(Extend your hand. Then silence. First one to talk owns the next move, and it will not be you.)

09

Objection Playbook

The 90% close

An objection is a buying signal. It's a rung on the ladder, not a wall. 99% of all objections come down to time or money. Stay calm, agree first, then climb.

The 6 stages. Run every objection through this, every time.The Engine
  • 1 · SILENCE. Let them finish, then pause 4 to 6 seconds. Never jump on an objection. The pause moves them from arguing back to feeling.
  • 2 · AFFIRM. "I can appreciate that." Agree it's worth considering. They braced for a fight and didn't get one.
  • 3 · QUESTION. "Could you share with me why you feel that way?" Then ask the other party too. A third of the time the spouse closes them for you.
  • 4 · ISOLATE. "Other than that, is there anything else standing between you and enjoying your new roof?" Pin it down so a second objection can't pop up after you solve the first.
  • 5 · SOLVE. Match the close to the real objection. Never drop price to fix procrastination.
  • 6 · CLOSE. Ask for the order again, then be quiet. Don't over-answer. "Fair enough? Let me get the paperwork out of the way."

Stages 3 and 4 are the heart of it: question and isolate. Your whole job is converting an excuse (think, shop, sleep on it) into the real objection, which is almost always money. You can close on money. You can't close on fog.

They said no to the first price.The Walk-Down

Don't panic and don't drop the number. Isolate every single piece, one small yes at a time, until the only thing left is the overall figure. Each question is a commitment statement, so collect them slowly:

Walk it down, piece by piece

"Let me make sure I understand where we are. This roof is something you have to do, right? That's not something we have to think over?"

"And Square Foot is still who you'd want doing your project?"

"The system itself, that's something you'd want on your home, right?"

"And you know when you do this, you don't want to do it twice, right?"

"The monthly payment, that's something that still fits?"

"How about the down payment, that's fine too?"

(Every yes shrinks the objection. Now finish it:)

"So it's really just the overall number. Fair enough? Then let me show you why that number is exactly right, and what we can do about it tonight."

The rebuttal · Only after you've questioned and isolated

"It is a big investment, and don't you agree you get what you pay for? We'd rather explain our price once than apologize for cheap work for the next twenty years. Doesn't it make sense to invest a little more in the best value for your family, instead of not spending enough and buying the same roof twice?"

(This lands as agreement now, because they just told you everything else is a yes. Said any earlier, it lands as an argument.)

From here go to the money math ($5 a day, below), or the today incentive from Step 8. If a specific piece got a "no" instead, that's your real objection. Solve that one and only that one.

"I need to get 3 quotes."ABC Method

Don't fight it. Reframe it. Draw the three quotes out for them:

Company A
$36k
Paying for their TV ads
Company B
$30k
Best value. That's us
Company C
$23k
Too good to be true
Say it like this

"Let's say your three quotes come back. Company A at $36,000, Company B at $30,000, Company C at $23,000. Same house. Who are you going with?"

(They almost never pick the cheapest.)

"Right. Nobody touches the $23k, because when it's that low something got left out: thinner shingles, no real warranty, an unlicensed crew, or a surprise wood bill on install day. And you're not paying $36k for the same roof either. You land in the middle, the best value for your family. That's exactly where Square Foot lives. So you already know how the three quotes end. Let's save you the two weekends and get you scheduled."

You can't close on "three bids." You can only close on money. This turns the stall into a price conversation, and you win on value and payment.

"I need to talk to my spouse."Hope Question

(This is why you fought to get both decision-makers at the table in Step 1. If one is truly out, don't argue. Get curious.)

Say it like this

"Totally fair, this is a big decision and you two make it together. Let me ask you though: when you tell them about it tonight, what are you hoping they'll say?"

(Now they sell it back to you, in their own words.)

"Sounds like you already know what you want to do. So let's do this: let's get it on paper at tonight's pricing so nothing changes, and you two have a few days to make 100% sure together. If either of you has a second thought, one call and it's undone. Fair?"

"It's a lot. Let me think about it."Isolate → Price vs. Cost

"Think about it" almost always means more information, not more time. Isolate it first:

Isolate it

"I hear you. Just so I help the right way, are you thinking about whether to do the roof at all, or about who to do it with? ... Or is it really just the number?"

(It's almost always the number. Then:)

Then reframe price vs. cost

"Here's the thing. Price is what you pay once. Cost is what you pay over and over. The cheap roof is actually the expensive one, paid back in leaks, repairs, and worry. And doing nothing is the priciest option of all, it just bills you slowly. You're going to do this roof anyway. The only question is whether you do it right, at tonight's price and payment. Let's get it started."

Never argue the whole price. You already have their number from the expectation question back in Step 5. Argue only the gap between your price and their wish number, then shrink that gap until it's almost funny:

Close on the difference

"So let's be honest about what's actually standing between us. It's not $18,000 versus zero, it's $18,000 versus the $15,000 you had in mind. That gap, spread over the life of this roof, is less than a dollar a day. Would you really let less than a buck a day stand between your family and the best roof in Hampton Roads, on something you're doing anyway?"

10

Post-Close · Sell It Back · Replace The Lead

Cancellation armor · Referrals · 5 stars

The moment they say yes, stop selling and make them sell it. The words they say now are the words they'll repeat to the neighbor who asks "why didn't you go with the cheap guy?" If you say it, they can doubt it. If they say it, it's true, and they don't cancel tomorrow.

Right after the yes · Then shut up

"Before I write this up, do me one favor. In your own words, why did you two decide to move forward tonight?"

"And if your neighbor leans over the fence and asks why you didn't just take the cheapest bid, what do you tell them?"

(Let them build it. Then lock it:) "That's exactly right, and exactly why it's a smart call. Welcome to the Square Foot family."

  • Paperwork while the yes is warm. All decision-makers initialing as you explain each page. No marathon sign-here-sign-here.
  • The cancellation form: be dead honest about it. "To be honest with you, this is the cancellation form. And if you even have the idea of using it, let's just stop the project right now, so we don't waste your time or your money." Said with a smile, it does two jobs at once: it proves you have nothing to hide, and it forces any hesitation onto the table while you're still sitting there.
  • If anything surfaces, deal with it now, while you're still in the home: "Is there any hesitation at all? Let's talk about it now." A doubt handled tonight is a cancellation that never happens tomorrow.
  • Warm them back down. Walk the house once more, set the timeline, tell them exactly who calls next. The last thing they remember should be gratitude, not being closed.
Replace the lead · The 5-star ask

"You just told me why this was right. Who's the first person you know who deserves the same free, honest look you got tonight? Costs them nothing, same as it did you."

"And regardless of pricing, which I don't control, was your experience with Square Foot a 5-star experience today?"

And if it's not a sale, still change the end

"Thank you for having me in your home. Even if we didn't earn your business tonight, I appreciate the chance to show you a little about Square Foot Roofing."
